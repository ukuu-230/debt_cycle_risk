Composite decomposition

flag value ∈ [0,100], regime-conditional research weights (renormalized over present flags)

Flags are normalized to a common risk scale (Fed/OFR stress-index construction), oriented so higher = more risk, and combined under fixed, regime-conditional weights drawn from external literature (BIS early-warning work; Adrian-Estrella-Shin term spread; Fed conditions indices), never estimated on the sample's ~8 recessions. The regime (calm/building/elevated) is set by the credit-gap level against Basel thresholds.

Cross-country credit-to-GDP gap

BIS ratios · one-sided HP λ=400k

The gap is the one non-redundant cross-country signal: term spreads and HY OAS co-move globally (already in the US composite), whereas credit cycles are national. Read the relative ordering more than the absolute levels: a one-sided HP trend anchored on the pre-2008 peak biases post-crisis gaps negative across the board.

Method notes

  • No look-ahead. Percentiles rank each value against prior observations only (mid-rank ties); the HP trend is one-sided (λ=400,000, 40q burn-in). Quarterly macro inputs are re-timed to their publication quarter (credit gap +2q, DSR +2q, M2 velocity +1q), and backtest quarters whose outcome window is not yet observed are excluded.
  • Stationary inputs. Trending series are transformed before ranking: M2 velocity enters as its year-on-year change; the long-bond yield is ranked within a trailing 10-year window. Curve: 10y−3m.
  • Specification v2 (Oct 2026). The credit-spread level was moved out of the structural composite into the short-term stress gauge: spreads are coincident (wide in and after recessions, tight before them), as the design notes stated. This was decided after the live backtest showed it (standalone AUC 0.40), so the v1 numbers are kept in the robustness table: v1 is the clean validated figure, v2 the better-designed gauge. Remaining weights are v1's renormalized; none was re-chosen.
  • No fitting to the target. Weights are external-research priors, not estimates, so the AUC is not an in-sample fit. A supervised stage classifier failed to beat its base rate and was demoted.
  • Metric-gap audit. 20 further candidates (fiscal, valuation, external, credit-supply, housing) are tested for lead AUC, redundancy and incremental ΔAUC under pre-registered rules, each re-timed to its publication date (run_metric_audit.py). The fiscal/valuation leg the backtest cannot validate is shown as context above.
  • Renormalization. Missing flags drop and weights renormalize; the headline uses the latest quarter with ≥ 4 flags.

Known limitations

  • Latest-vintage data. Current-vintage FRED, not ALFRED point-in-time: publication timing is corrected, but later data revisions still leak into the backtest.
  • HP-filter critique. The one-sided HP gap is the Basel/BIS standard, but Hamilton (2018) shows it can generate spurious cycle dynamics; read gap magnitudes with that in mind.
  • Small sample. ~8 non-independent recessions. AUCs are descriptive, not inferential.
  • No non-US validation. NBER is US-only; the cross-country gaps carry no recession backtest. Short-history gaps are marked by confidence tier.
  • Exogenous blindness by design. It models credit cycles, not shocks (2001 and 2020 stay quiet). Not financial advice.

FRAMEWORK: Dalio, Principles for Navigating Big Debt Crises; How Countries Go Broke. DATA: FRED (Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis), Bank for International Settlements, OECD. NBER chronology via USREC.
